From Students, For Students is a workshop series built around practical skills for modern research.

Each workshop is led by PhD students sharing the tools, methods, and workflows they use in their own projects - the resources and lessons that took time to find, understand, and integrate into daily research work.

Whether you are just beginning graduate school or approaching the end of your degree, we hope you will discover something useful, exchange ideas with your peers, and gain skills you can apply to your own research.

Bring your laptop, your questions, and join us for an interactive session of learning, discussion, and scientific collaboration.

Each session runs concurrently.

Participants select one workshop per session during registration.

Applied Statistics in R: From Data to Results

Hands-on · Bring your laptop · All levels welcome

This hands-on workshop introduces practical statistical methods commonly used in agricultural research using R. Through real-world agronomic datasets, participants will explore correlation analysis, linear regression, nonlinear regression, and analysis of variance (ANOVA).

While examples will focus on agronomy and soil fertility research, the concepts and analytical approaches are broadly applicable across agricultural and biological sciences. The workshop emphasizes understanding statistical concepts, interpreting results, and generating publication-quality outputs rather than learning programming syntax.

Using partially developed R scripts and guided examples, participants will analyze data, interpret statistical results, and create figures suitable for scientific communication.

Artificial intelligence tools will also be demonstrated as resources for explaining code, troubleshooting errors, and adapting scripts to different research applications. By the end of the workshop, participants will have completed several analyses and generated results and figures suitable for theses, reports, and scientific publications.

Turning Field and Lab Measurements into Clean Datasets.

Hands-on · Bring your laptop · All levels welcome

Research data in agricultural experiments are often collected across field and lab activities using different formats, inconsistent structures, and manual steps. These practices can lead to errors, duplicated records, missing information, and confusion when students and researchers prepare data for statistical analysis. In many cases, organizing raw measurements into datasets ready for analysis becomes time-consuming and inefficient.

In this workshop, participants will explore a practical workflow for organized research data collection, processing, quality control, and automated reporting. Through applied examples, we will discuss how structured data entry, sample traceability, and automated processing can reduce errors, improve efficiency, and reduce, at last, labor requirements in research programs. Examples will include green canopy cover estimation from drone images, thousand kernel weight estimation from grain pictures, and automated weather data retrieval during the growing season. Participants will also explore basic data checks, visualization, and automatic report generation.

Working Smarter with AI: Practical Tools and Critical Thinking for Scientific Data Analysis

Hands-on · Bring your laptop · All levels welcome

Generative AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot are rapidly entering scientific workflows - but how do we use them well? This workshop offers a practical, grounded perspective on integrating AI into data analysis, with an emphasis on efficiency, transparency, and critical thinking.

What we will cover:

  • Live demonstration: using AI for data exploration and interpretation.
  • Hands-on exercise: how prompt design shapes results and why that matters.
  • Best practices: when to automate, when to question, and how to stay in control of your analysis.
  • A framework for responsible AI use in scientific publishing and reporting.

By the end of this workshop, participants will have a sense of when AI helps, when it misleads, and how to tell the difference.

Analyzing Biological Processes Over Time: From Field Observations to Decisions

Hands-on · Bring your laptop · Intermediate R and statistics recommended

In plant sciences, many biological processes develop over time, generating repeated measurements that describe growth, disease development, and responses to management practices. In plant pathology, epidemic progress is commonly monitored throughout the season to understand how management decisions influence disease development. These data are often collected in designed field experiments, where treatments are applied and evaluated through repeated observations over time.

In this workshop, participants will follow the complete process of answering a research question using disease progress data from field experiments.

Through practical examples, we will explore approaches for modeling and comparing curves, extracting biologically meaningful quantities, and evaluating treatment effects. The goal is to provide participants with a practical framework for interpreting temporal data and translating experimental observations into informed decisions.

Examples and demonstrations will be conducted using R. Participants may follow along in R or use the provided workshop materials.
